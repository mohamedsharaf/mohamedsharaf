The Model Context Protocol, MCP, is how AI assistants like Claude connect to other tools: your files, GitHub, a database, your calendar. Adding one takes a minute. That’s the problem.

An MCP server is a program. It runs with your access, sees what you ask the assistant, and returns text the assistant will trust. A bad one can read files it shouldn’t, or slip instructions into its replies.

Before adding one:

  • Prefer servers from the vendor itself or a well-known source, and look at who maintains it.
  • Check what it can do. A read-only tool is safer than one that writes or deletes.
  • Give it a token with narrow scope, not your personal admin key.
  • Pin the version, and read the changes before updating.
  • Remove the ones you stopped using.

In a company, keep a short approved list. Otherwise everyone installs their own, and nobody knows what’s connected to what.
