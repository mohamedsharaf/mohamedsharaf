I’ve seen AI policies that run to twenty pages. Nobody reads them, so people keep doing what they were doing. A one-page version that answers the real questions works better. Here’s the shape I use:

  • Approved tools: the one or two AI services the company pays for, and how to get access.
  • Never put in any AI tool: passwords and keys, ID and card numbers, health data, client data unless the contract allows it.
  • Always check: facts, numbers, names and code before they go to a client or into production. You’re responsible for the output.
  • Say so: when content sent to clients was mainly AI-written, if the client expects to know.
  • Ask first: before connecting AI tools to company email, files or systems.
  • Who to ask: one named person.

Review it every six months. The tools change faster than the policy.
