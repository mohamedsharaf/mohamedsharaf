The same week Chainalysis was tracing the $6.3 billion Binance theft, it disclosed a breach of its own. On 30 October the company said an outside attacker using “offensive agentic techniques”, in other words attacks driven by AI agents, got into its systems and internal tools this month (Chainalysis).

Chainalysis says it isn’t related to Binance. What was taken: user IDs, full names, email addresses and organisation role labels. The company says it has no evidence that investigation content, query results, passwords, MFA secrets or session tokens were accessed.

“Only names and emails” sounds minor. It isn’t. Chainalysis’s customers include government agencies, exchanges and banks, so the list shows who investigates crypto crime, where, and in what role. That’s a ready-made target list for phishing. This week, a fake “Binance case update” would look very believable.

If your team uses Chainalysis, or any similar platform:

  • Warn the affected users today. Expect emails that use their real name, their role and a real case from the news.
  • Treat unexpected password resets and MFA prompts as attacks until proven otherwise.
  • Move to phishing-resistant MFA: passkeys or hardware security keys.
  • Check which of your staff have accounts on outside platforms, and remove the people who have left.

Chainalysis says it is now using large language models to review its own platform’s security. If attackers use AI to look for weak spots, defenders should be looking first.
