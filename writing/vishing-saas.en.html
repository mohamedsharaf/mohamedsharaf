Two breaches reported this month follow the same pattern. At Crunchyroll, an attacker reportedly phished an agent at an outsourced support provider who had single sign-on access to internal tools, and downloaded millions of support tickets. At Aura, an identity protection company, a voice phishing call to an employee led to access to about 900,000 records.

No exploit, no malware. A convincing call, an MFA prompt approved, and a SaaS export button.

  • Use phishing-resistant MFA, passkeys or security keys, for staff and contractors with access to customer data. Push approvals and SMS codes can be talked out of people.
  • Limit what support agents can see and export. Most don’t need bulk downloads.
  • Alert on large exports from your CRM and support tools.
  • Tell staff plainly: IT will never call and ask you to approve a login.
