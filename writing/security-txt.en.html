Every few months someone emails info@ or messages a random employee on LinkedIn: “I found a security problem on your website.” Sometimes it’s a scam. Often it’s real, and the message sits unread.

A security.txt file tells researchers where to send reports. It lives at /.well-known/security.txt and can be as short as:

Contact: mailto:security@example.com
Expires: 2027-03-31T00:00:00Z

A few things make it work:

  • Use an address that a real person reads, and reply within a few days, even just to say thanks.
  • Add a short page that says what you consider in scope and that you won’t take legal action against good-faith reports.
  • Update the Expires date every year. A stale file looks abandoned.

You don’t need a bug bounty programme to start. You need a door that someone answers.
