Many companies run phishing simulations in October. Done badly, they send a trick email, count who clicked, and enrol those people in a boring course. Staff learn one lesson: if you click something, don’t tell anyone.

That’s the opposite of what you need. In a real attack, the fastest report is worth more than the click rate.

How I’d run them:

  • Measure reports, not just clicks. The goal is a higher report rate and a faster first report.
  • Use realistic lures from this year: shared document, MFA reset call follow-up, delivery notice, invoice change.
  • When someone clicks, show a short, friendly page explaining the signs. No public lists, no punishment.
  • Don’t use cruel lures, like fake bonuses or layoffs. They destroy trust.
  • Share overall results with everyone, and celebrate the people who reported first.
