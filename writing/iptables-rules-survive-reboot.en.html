A classic surprise: you add firewall rules with iptables, everything works, the server reboots, and the rules are gone. They only lived in memory.

On CentOS 6 and 7 (with the iptables-services package), tell the service to save the rules when it stops or restarts, in /etc/sysconfig/iptables-config:

IPTABLES_SAVE_ON_STOP="yes"
IPTABLES_SAVE_ON_RESTART="yes"

Then save the current rules and make sure the service starts at boot:

service iptables save
chkconfig iptables on        # CentOS 6
systemctl enable iptables    # CentOS 7

Today: newer distributions use firewalld or nftables, where you make rules permanent with firewall-cmd --permanent or by saving the nftables ruleset. Whatever the tool, test a reboot before you trust it.
