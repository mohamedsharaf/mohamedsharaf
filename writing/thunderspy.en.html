This week a security researcher published Thunderspy: flaws in Thunderbolt that let someone with a few minutes alone with a laptop read its memory and get around the lock screen, even with disk encryption on. It mainly affects computers made before 2019 without a feature called Kernel DMA Protection.

Most of us aren’t targets of a hotel-room attack. But physical access is easy to forget:

  • Shut down or hibernate laptops when you leave them somewhere, rather than just locking the screen.
  • Use full disk encryption with a PIN at startup on sensitive machines.
  • Check Kernel DMA Protection in Windows System Information. Newer laptops have it.
  • Update firmware from your laptop maker.
  • Disable Thunderbolt in the BIOS on high-risk machines that don’t use it.
