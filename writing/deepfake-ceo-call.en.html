This year an employee at an engineering firm’s Hong Kong office sent about 25 million US dollars to criminals. The request came on a video call with the company’s CFO and several colleagues. All of them were deepfakes. The firm, Arup, confirmed it in May.

A cloned voice now needs a few seconds of audio. A convincing face on a call is getting cheap. “I saw them on video” isn’t proof anymore.

The defence isn’t a detection tool. It’s a payment process nobody can skip:

  1. Any new payee or changed bank details get confirmed by calling back on a number you already have, never one from the message or call.
  2. Large payments need two people, and one of them wasn’t on the call.
  3. Urgency and secrecy are red flags, especially together. “Don’t tell anyone, it’s confidential, do it today.”
  4. Agree a code word between finance and leadership for unusual requests.

And make it clear that following the process is never insubordination, even if the “CEO” is shouting.
