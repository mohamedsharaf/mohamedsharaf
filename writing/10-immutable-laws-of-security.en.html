In 2000, Scott Culp at Microsoft’s Security Response Center wrote down ten “laws”: problems no vendor can patch, because they come from how computers work. In short:

  1. If someone can get you to run their program, your computer isn’t only yours anymore.
  2. If someone can change your operating system, it isn’t your computer anymore.
  3. If someone has unrestricted physical access, it isn’t your computer anymore.
  4. If you let someone upload programs to your website, it isn’t your website anymore.
  5. Weak passwords beat strong security.
  6. A computer is only as secure as its administrator is trustworthy.
  7. Encrypted data is only as secure as the key that unlocks it.
  8. An out-of-date antivirus is barely better than none.
  9. Complete anonymity isn’t practical.
  10. Technology is not a cure-all.

Twenty-five years later, almost every incident I hear about still maps to one of these. Law 4 is the reason I wrote about file uploads, and law 10 is the one people forget most: tools help, but people and process decide.

Read the original: 10 Immutable Laws of Security, by Scott Culp, Microsoft (2000).
