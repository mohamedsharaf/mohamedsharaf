Every incident starts with the same question: what happened, and since when? Without logs, the honest answer is “we don’t know”, and that’s the worst answer to give a client.

Keep at least these:

  • Logins: success and failure, with IP and time, for email, VPN, admin panels and servers.
  • Admin actions: who created users, changed permissions, deleted data.
  • Web server and firewall logs for anything facing the internet.
  • Cloud audit logs. Most providers have them; some are off by default.

Three rules:

  1. Send them somewhere else. An attacker on the server can delete local logs.
  2. Keep them long enough. Break-ins are often found months later. 90 days is a minimum; a year is better.
  3. Sync the clocks. Logs with wrong times can’t be matched up.

You don’t have to read them every day. Just alert on a few things: many failed logins, a new admin, logins from a new country.
