The classic way to reach an office server from outside is to forward a port on the router: 3389 for remote desktop, 443 for a web app, 22 for SSH. That service is now visible to every scanner on the internet, all day.

A tunnel works the other way round. A small agent on your network connects out to a provider, such as Cloudflare Tunnel, Tailscale or a similar service, and the provider only lets authenticated users through. No inbound ports open on your router at all.

  • Put an identity check in front: your company login with MFA, not just a shared password.
  • Allow access per application and per group, instead of a whole network.
  • Close the old forwarded ports once the tunnel works, and scan from outside to confirm.
  • Log who connected to what. It’s usually built in.

I now set up every small office and home lab this way. It’s safer, and there’s no static IP to manage.
