Mike Jolley, a core developer of WooCommerce, argued in 2013 that most plugin vulnerabilities come from skipping a few easy habits. Studies at the time found a large share of popular plugins vulnerable to common attacks. The habits, in short:

  • Develop with WP_DEBUG on, so warnings and notices show up while you build, not in your users’ logs.
  • Sanitise input: sanitize_text_field(), absint(), sanitize_email() and friends on everything from $_GET, $_POST and cookies.
  • Escape output: esc_html(), esc_attr(), esc_url() at the moment you print.
  • Use nonces on every form and action that changes something (wp_nonce_field() and check_admin_referer()).
  • Check capabilities with current_user_can(), not just “is logged in”.
  • Prepare SQL: $wpdb->prepare() for every query with a variable.
  • Block direct access to PHP files: defined( 'ABSPATH' ) || exit; at the top.

None of this is hard. It just has to be a habit, every time.
