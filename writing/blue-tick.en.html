Last week anyone could buy Twitter’s blue check for eight dollars, and within days “verified” accounts were impersonating brands. One fake Eli Lilly account announced free insulin; the real company’s stock dipped and it had to apologise for a tweet it never wrote. Twitter paused the scheme.

The lesson goes beyond Twitter: a badge only means what the platform checks before giving it. If it can be bought, it says you paid.

For individuals:

  • Don’t trust a badge for customer support, investment tips or giveaways. Go to the company’s website and follow its official links.
  • Never move to DMs to “fix your account” or “claim a refund”.

For companies:

  • List your official accounts on your website, so people can check.
  • Watch for impersonation of your brand and executives, and report it fast.
  • Tell customers how you’ll contact them, and how you never will.
