At the 2007 MySQL Conference, Paul Tuckfield, a long-time sysadmin and DBA at YouTube, told the story of keeping the site’s databases ahead of its explosive growth. The lessons that stuck with me:

  • The fastest query is the one you don’t run. Caching (memcached in front of MySQL) did more than any database tuning.
  • Reads far outnumber writes, so replication, with many read copies of one primary, carried them a long way.
  • Replication has limits: lag between primary and replicas, and every replica still has to apply every write. Eventually the data has to be split (sharding).
  • Know the layers: RAID controllers, the operating system’s cache and the database’s own cache can fight each other. Understanding where data actually sits in memory matters.

I ran large real-time systems myself around the same time, and the same truth held: plan for the busiest night, keep the setup simple enough to fix under pressure, and don’t ask the database for anything you can avoid.
