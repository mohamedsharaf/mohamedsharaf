Small businesses often think they are too small to be a target. Most attacks aren’t targeted, though. They are automated: stolen passwords tried everywhere, old software scanned for known holes, phishing emails sent to thousands. You don’t need a security team to stop most of that. You need the basics, done properly.

The UK’s Cyber Essentials scheme is built on five technical controls, and they are a good map for any small company: firewalls, secure configuration, access control, malware protection and security updates. Here is how I would put them into practice, in order.

1. Lock down the accounts

  • Turn on multi-factor authentication for email, cloud storage, accounting and any admin panel. Email first: it resets everything else.
  • Use a password manager for the whole team, and unique passwords everywhere.
  • Remove accounts of people who left, the same day they leave.

2. Keep everything updated

  • Turn on automatic updates for operating systems, browsers and office software.
  • Make a list of anything that faces the internet: website, VPN, firewall, remote access. These get patched first, within days, not months.
  • Replace software and devices that no longer receive updates.

3. Give people only what they need

  • Daily work happens in normal accounts, not administrator accounts.
  • Shared folders are shared with the people who need them, not “everyone with the link”.
  • Finance and HR data sit behind the strongest controls.

4. Protect the devices and the network

  • Built-in protection is a fine start: the firewall and antivirus that come with modern Windows and macOS, switched on and up to date.
  • Encrypt laptop disks, so a lost laptop is only a hardware cost.
  • Change default passwords on routers, cameras and printers, and don’t expose remote desktop directly to the internet.

5. Have backups you have restored

  • Keep at least one copy that ransomware can’t reach or delete, and test a restore regularly.

And one habit

Make it easy and safe to report mistakes. “I clicked something odd” reported in five minutes is a small problem. Hidden for two weeks, it is a big one. Thank people who report, every time.

None of this is expensive. Most of it is settings and habits. Done together, these basics stop the majority of what actually happens to small companies.
