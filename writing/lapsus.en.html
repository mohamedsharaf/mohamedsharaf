In a few months, a young group called Lapsus$ got into Nvidia, Samsung, Microsoft and, last week, a support contractor working for Okta. Microsoft’s write-up of their methods is worth reading because there’s nothing exotic in it:

  • Buying passwords and session tokens from infostealer markets.
  • Spamming MFA prompts until someone approves.
  • SIM swapping to take over phone numbers used for codes.
  • Openly offering to pay employees for access.
  • Then joining the company’s own chat and calls to watch the response.

The defences match:

  1. Phishing-resistant MFA such as security keys for admins and support staff. No prompts to spam, no codes to steal.
  2. Move away from SMS codes.
  3. Strong checks for help desk resets and new MFA devices.
  4. Treat contractors’ access like your own staff’s: same MFA, same logging, least access.
  5. Run incident calls on a separate channel when you suspect your main one is watched.

And tell your staff that anyone offering money for access should be reported. It sounds obvious. It worked for Lapsus$.
