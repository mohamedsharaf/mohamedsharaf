Most attacks on small companies aren’t targeted. They’re automated: leaked passwords tried everywhere, old software scanned for known holes. The basics stop most of it. In order:

  1. Accounts. MFA on email first, then cloud storage, accounting and every admin panel. A password manager for everyone. Remove leavers the day they leave.
  2. Updates. Automatic updates on. Anything facing the internet (website, VPN, firewall, remote access) gets patched within days. Replace what no longer gets updates.
  3. Access. Daily work in normal accounts, not admin. Folders shared with people, not “anyone with the link”.
  4. Devices. Built-in firewall and antivirus on and current. Disk encryption on laptops. Default passwords changed on routers and cameras. No remote desktop open to the internet.
  5. Backups. One copy ransomware can’t delete, and a restore you’ve actually tested.

The UK’s Cyber Essentials uses almost the same list, if you want a checklist to follow.

Last thing: make it safe to say “I clicked something weird”. Five minutes later it’s a small problem. Two weeks later it isn’t.
