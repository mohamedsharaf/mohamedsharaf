Ledger, the hardware wallet maker, told customers this month that order data, names and contact details, was exposed through a breach at Global-e, a partner that runs parts of its online store. Ledger’s own systems weren’t the problem.

For a crypto wallet company, a list of buyers with home addresses is exactly what scammers want. Expect phishing emails and calls that quote real order details.

Most online shops have the same setup: payment provider, shipping company, email marketing tool, support desk. Each one holds a copy of your customers.

  • List the partners that receive customer data, and what each one gets.
  • Send them less. The shipping company needs an address, not a purchase history.
  • Ask how they’ll notify you of a breach, and how fast.
  • Prepare a short customer message in advance, so you can warn people the same day.

Customers don’t care whose server it was. They gave the data to you.
