Last week Uber was breached, and the attacker posted about it in Uber’s own Slack. The chain, as reported:

  1. A contractor’s password, probably bought from a criminal market.
  2. Repeated MFA push prompts, plus a message on WhatsApp claiming to be Uber IT and asking to approve. Eventually it was approved.
  3. Inside the network, a file share with a PowerShell script containing admin credentials for the company’s password vault system.

From there, the keys to much of Uber.

Each step has a known fix:

  • Number matching or security keys instead of simple “Approve” prompts.
  • A clear rule: IT never asks you to approve a login by chat or phone.
  • Search your shares and repos for passwords in scripts. There are tools that do this.
  • Protect the vault with the strongest MFA and the fewest admins you have.

The attacker wasn’t sophisticated. The gaps were common. That should worry everyone a bit more than a fancy exploit would.
